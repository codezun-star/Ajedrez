# Chess Opening Principles Every Beginner Should Know

You do not need to memorize long opening lines to play a great opening. If you
follow a handful of principles, you will reach a solid middlegame in almost every
game.

## Control the center

The four central squares — d4, e4, d5, e5 — are the most valuable real estate on
the board. A piece in the center controls more squares and attacks in more
directions. Start with a central pawn move like **1.e4** or **1.d4**.

## Develop your pieces

Bring your knights and bishops into play before you attack. Two quick rules:

- **Knights before bishops** as a general habit.
- **Don't move the same piece twice** in the opening without a reason.

Bringing the queen out too early is a classic trap: your opponent develops with
tempo by attacking her.

## Castle early

Castling does two good things at once — it tucks your king safely into the corner
and connects your rooks. Aim to castle within the first ten moves.

## Don't grab pawns too greedily

Winning a pawn while your pieces sit undeveloped often backfires. Development and
king safety are usually worth more than a single pawn in the opening.

## Watch your opponent's threats

Before every move, ask: **what does my opponent want to do?** Most beginner losses
come from missing a simple threat — a hanging piece, a check, or a knight fork.

## Put it together

A model opening might look like: **1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4 Bc5 4.O-O** — center
staked out, pieces developed, king castled. Nothing fancy, just good principles.

---

The best way to internalize these ideas is to play. Start a game against the AI,
try to follow every principle, then review your PGN to see where you drifted.
